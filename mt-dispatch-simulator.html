Model assumptions — argue with these

Weekday demand: ~58% of leads in the 7–10am burst, ~27% in the 4–7pm burst, rest scattered. Weekend demand: all-day plateau 9am–7pm at ~1.25× hot-weekday volume — people are home discovering failures continuously, and they're home for the repair.

Thin competition: on weekends and after 6pm, win odds get a 1.35× boost (capped 92%) — half the vans went home. This is the HomeAdvisor-Saturday effect.

A · Traditional: both trucks Mon–Fri 8–4, weekends off. Jason's mornings carry FM (8–11). Stay-till-fixed visits. No deposit, no texts: 15–25% ghost, 60% of ghosts = dry runs. Weekend and evening callers get "Monday" — 10% stick. Pays for every lead. No premium charged.

B · Burst/Split: Mon–Fri Brian 8–1 + 4–8, Jason 10–6, FM in the 1–4 dead zone; Sat half-day (Brian 8–1), Sun off. Sweep model, paid lock + text chain (5% ghost, 90% caught), evening premium, LSA pauses when board can't offer inside 2.5 hrs.

C · Contrarian: Jason runs weekdays solo 8–4 (sweep model, FM 1–4). Brian works weekends only, 8am–8pm — full emergency calls, diagnose-and-repair same visit when parts are on the truck, premium rates all day. ~24 Brian hours/week, exactly the HomeAdvisor pattern. Same paid-lock + text stack. Weekday evening callers get a locked next-morning slot.

Money: $89 diagnostic per completed visit, ticket ±40% around the average, premium applies to evening (5–8p) and all weekend repairs for B and C. FM billed $140/hr, 3 hrs every weekday in all three schedules — the gap you see is capture, waste, and premium, never dropped FM.

Fairness: each simulated customer's patience, ghost tendency, close decision, parts-on-truck, and ticket size are drawn once and shared by all three schedules.
