Model assumptions — argue with these

Lead timing: ~58% of leads land in the 7–10am burst, ~27% in the 4–7pm burst, ~15% scattered midday. Lead volume scales with temperature; mild days run ~25% of hot-day volume.

Win odds vs offered arrival: under 30 min ≈ 85%, 1 hr ≈ 72%, 2 hr ≈ 50%, 3 hr ≈ 30%, 5+ hr ≈ 5%. This is the 2-hour-window insight turned into a curve.

Schedule A (Traditional): both trucks 8–4. Jason's mornings carry 3 hrs of FM work (8–11). Full-repair model — truck stays through the fix when parts are on the truck (50% of jobs), so calls stack behind long visits. No paid diagnostic, no confirm texts: 15–25% of booked jobs ghost, and 60% of ghosts become dry runs. Evening callers get "tomorrow" — ~18% stick. Pays for every lead.

Schedule B (Burst): Brian 8–1 + 4–8, Jason 10–6 with FM moved to the 1–4 dead zone. Sweep model — diagnose, quote, bounce; repairs stack into afternoon/evening blocks and next-morning pre-slots. Paid diagnostic + confirm texts: ghosting drops to ~5% and texts catch 90% of it before the truck rolls. Evening leads get tonight (premium) or a locked, paid 8am slot (~50% stick). LSA pauses when the board can't offer inside 2.5 hrs — those leads are never paid for.

Money: $89 diagnostic on every completed visit, repair ticket ±40% around the average, FM billed at $140/hr — both schedules work the same 3 FM hrs/day, just placed differently. That's deliberate: the revenue gap you see comes from capture, waste, and premium, not from dropping FM.

Fairness: each simulated customer's willingness to wait, ghost tendency, close decision, parts-on-truck, and ticket size are drawn once and shared by both schedules.
