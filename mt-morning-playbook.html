Structure

Morning Rules (Cooling Season)

Forecast 85°+ → mornings stay empty.No PMs, no FM work orders, no repairs before 11am. The empty slot IS the product — it's what lets you say "45 minutes" at 8:30.
Two trucks, two diagnostics.Jason takes one, Brian takes the other. Morning visits are 30–45 min diagnostic sweeps — diagnose, quote, get approval, book the repair, bounce. Repairs stack in the afternoon.
Callback inside 3 minutes. Book inside 90 seconds.Lock the slot first. Equipment details come by text after. Every question you ask on call #1 is time they spend answering another contractor.
Quote a range, anchored early."Between 9:30 and 11" beats "11am." Customers hear the front of the range. Never pad to the safe end — 1pm on an 8am call is a lost job.
Board full → pause LSA.Once both trucks are committed and you're quoting past the 2-hour window, every new lead is $88 burned. Pause it. Two leads you win beat four you split.
Slow morning = droppable work only.Part runs, quote follow-ups, callbacks. Nothing you can't abandon the second the phone rings.
Script 1

The Callback

Goal: locked slot + verbal contract, under 90 seconds. Blue = say it. Red = the hook that makes bailing feel like breaking a promise.

Open & claim0:00–0:20
Say it"Hey, this is Brian with Mechanical Temp — you just called about your AC. I can have a tech at your place between [time] and [time] this morning. Want me to lock that in right now?"
Why: You're the owner calling back in 3 minutes with a real window. No diagnostics questions yet. Lock first, details later.
Triage check0:20–0:35
Say it"Quick question — is the house getting dangerously hot? Anybody elderly, little kids, pets?"
Why: True emergency = highest-close, highest-ticket call of the day. If yes, this job can bump anything non-urgent on the board.
The lock — money down0:35–1:00
Say it"I'll get you on the board for [window]. I take the $89 diagnostic now by card — it comes straight off the repair — and that locks your slot. Nobody bumps you. I'll text you a payment link right now."
Why: Money down closes the auction. A customer with $89 committed doesn't take the 9:10 counter-offer. And whoever won't pay just told you they were always going to jump — before you built a route around them.
The guilt transfer1:00–1:20
The hook"One thing — I'm setting my whole morning around you now. If somebody else gets it fixed first, or you get it handled, no hard feelings at all — just shoot me a text so I don't drive out there for nothing. Deal?"
Why: Wait for them to actually say "deal" or "yeah." That word is the contract. Silent no-shows drop hard once they've promised a specific guy out loud. Worst case, you find out it's dead before you burn the drive.
The inoculation1:20–1:40
The hook"Heads up — you probably called a couple other places, so your phone's gonna ring for the next hour and some guy will say he can be there in 30 minutes. Most of those slip — that's just how they win the phone call. You're locked with me and I'll actually be there."
Why: You just predicted the exact counter-offer they're about to get. When it comes, it sounds like the sales tactic you called out — not a rescue. This is what protects the 8:35 booking from the 9:10 callback.
Read the closeListen
"Sounds great" + silence = backup plan. Committed customers ask questions — "do you take card," "what's the fee," "should I do anything before you get here." Instant polite agreement with zero questions means they're back on Google the second you hang up. That's your cue to push the payment link harder or mentally mark the slot as soft.

The Text Chain

Every gap of silence is a window for another contractor. These close the gaps. Tap to copy — swap the brackets.

T1 · Instantly at bookingYou're locked in with Mechanical Temp for [window] today. [Tech name] is your technician. Payment link for the $89 diagnostic (comes off your repair): [link]. Reply here anytime — this goes straight to Brian.
T2 · 90 min before windowMorning check-in from Mechanical Temp — [tech] is still on for your [window] window. Reply YES to confirm, or STOP if you're already taken care of so we don't send the truck.
T3 · Rolling / 10 min out[Tech] is about 10 minutes out. See you shortly — Mechanical Temp.
T4 · Left site, repair pendingHere's what happens next: part is ordered tonight, and you'll get a text in the morning with your repair time. You don't need to do anything — Brian, Mechanical Temp.
T5 · Same evening, job doneThanks for having us out today. If everything's cooling right, a quick Google review helps us a ton: [link]. Save this number — you're in our system now, so next time you skip the phone tree and come straight to us.
T2 is the dry-run killer. No reply or STOP = call before rolling the truck. T5 is the anti-LSA play — every direct repeat customer is a lead you didn't pay Google $88 for.
In the moment

If / Then

They say "let me check with my husband/wife and call you back"
"Totally — I'll hold the [window] slot for 15 minutes. After that it goes to the next call. Text me a thumbs up and you're locked." Deadline keeps you off the maybe pile.
They admit they already booked someone else
"No problem. If that guy slips — and a lot do on days like this — text this number and I'll fit you in. Save it either way." You just became the backup for when the 30-minute promise breaks.
They balk at paying the diagnostic up front
"No pressure — it just means I can't guarantee the slot if an emergency jumps the line. Want me to pencil you in as flexible instead?" Soft downgrade. Don't build a route around an unpaid slot.
They say they called a bunch of places
"Makes sense in this heat. If we're your guy, cancel the others so nobody wastes a trip — and we'll waive the diagnostic when you approve the repair." Names the behavior out loud, rewards exclusivity, only costs you on won jobs.
Lead #3 calls and both trucks are out
Quote first-available honestly: "I can be there at [time], and you're first in line if anything opens sooner — I'll text you." If this happens every heat wave, that's the truck-#3 math writing itself.
Board is full and it's not even 9am
Pause LSA. Every lead after full is $88 funding somebody else's rat race.
Experiment

Log It So It Locks In

This is a test, not gospel. One line per lead is enough to know in two weeks what's working:

Per lead: time in → time called back → window quoted → paid diagnostic Y/N → said "deal" Y/N → showed/cancelled/ghosted → won/lost → ticket $

What you're looking for: do paid-diagnostic leads ghost less? Does the "deal" hook cut dry runs? What window length still wins? If leads that pay never ghost, push the payment ask harder. If nobody pays but "deal" holds them, drop the fee and keep the hook. Two weeks of lines and the pattern's obvious — and it's a DSM table when you're ready.
